Hold'em Cheats

A supervillain's beginner guide to Texas Hold'em — for college students, friendly tables, and anyone who keeps donating their stack to "that guy."

A♥ K♠ Q♦ J♠ 10♥

Before you sit down

Every mastermind keeps a notebook. Ours has nothing illegal in it — just the rules, the rankings, and the handful of habits that separate players who last all night from players who refill their stack every ten minutes. Read the sheets, run the quiz, then go be mildly evil at a friendly table.

  1. Stakes you can laugh about

    Set a buy-in everyone can shrug at. Chips are for bragging rights, not rent money. If the pot stops being fun, that's the whole game stopping.

  2. Folding is a play, not a surrender

    The best villains know when to leave the scene. You will win more money by folding badly than by calling bravely.

  3. Cheat sheets are defaults, not laws

    Every poker decision depends on context: position, stack sizes, bet size, and who exactly is across the table. This guide gives you the starting answers; the quiz makes you adapt them.

"Cheats" here means strategy cheat sheets. No cards were marked, no dealers were bribed, and if anyone accuses you of an eyebrow trick, deny everything.

The Ten Hands, Best to Worst

Learn these in order. The dealer's job is to always have the best hand; your job is knowing when you don't. Each hand beats everything below it — that's the whole list.

  1. 1

    Royal Flush

    Villain file: The Masterplan

    Ten through ace, all the same suit. Five cards that cannot lose. It exists mostly to end arguments.

    10♥ J♥ Q♥ K♥ A♥
  2. 2

    Straight Flush

    Villain file: The Shadow Syndicate

    Five in a row, all the same suit. A straight and a flush at once — "almost royal" is still terrifying.

    5♠ 6♠ 7♠ 8♠ 9♠
  3. 3

    Four of a Kind

    Villain file: The Four Henchmen

    Four cards of the same rank. The leftover card (the "kicker") is just there for the photo.

    9♣ 9♦ 9♥ 9♠ K♦
  4. 4

    Full House

    Villain file: The Hostile Takeover

    Three of a kind plus a pair, like Q Q Q 4 4. Read it aloud: "queens full of fours."

    Q♠ Q♦ Q♥ 4♣ 4♠
  5. 5

    Flush

    Villain file: The Suit Syndicate

    Five cards of the same suit, in no particular order. Sequence is irrelevant; matching jackets are everything.

    2♥ 5♥ 8♥ J♥ K♥
  6. 6

    Straight

    Villain file: The March of Minions

    Five ranks in a row with mixed suits, like 5-6-7-8-9. The ace counts high (10-J-Q-K-A) or low (A-2-3-4-5).

    5♣ 6♦ 7♥ 8♠ 9♦
  7. 7

    Three of a Kind

    Villain file: The Trio of Terror

    Three cards of the same rank. When you make it with your own two cards, players call it a "set" — and players get nervous.

    J♣ J♦ J♥ 5♠ 9♦
  8. 8

    Two Pair

    Villain file: The Double Agents

    Two different pairs, like aces and sevens. If two players hold the same two pairs, the leftover card decides it.

    A♠ A♦ 7♣ 7♥ K♠
  9. 9

    One Pair

    Villain file: The Sidekicks

    Two cards of the same rank plus three loose ends. Your first real hand — and your most expensive crush.

    K♣ K♦ 8♠ 4♥ 2♦
  10. 10

    High Card

    Villain file: The Random Goon

    Nothing matches. If nobody pairs anything, the highest single card wins — which is why everyone secretly wants an ace.

    A♠ 9♦ 7♣ 4♥ 2♠

Ties split the pot. When two hands match, the leftover cards — "kickers" — decide who takes it: an ace kicker beats a king kicker every time.

Position: The Seat Is the Strategy

Poker isn't only what you hold — it's when you have to act. The same cards are a weapon in one seat and a trap in another.

  • Early & middle (UTG, MP): act first, five people still to come — keep your range tight.
  • Late (CO, BTN): act last after the flop — see what everyone does first, then decide. The best seats in the house.
  • Blinds (SB, BB): forced bets you posted before the cards came. Fine preflop, awkward afterward — you act first on every street.

Why acting last is power

After the flop, action starts left of the button and works around to the button. That means everyone else shows you their hand — bet, check, or fold — before you have to decide. Information first, decision last: that's what position buys.

In early position you're guessing: one raise behind you and your "decent" hand is suddenly expensive. In late position you're narrating: you know who's weak, who's stubborn, and how big the pot really is.

Context is king

Position is a head start, not a mind reader. Before you act, the situation still matters:

  • Who's left to act? One player behind you vs. four changes everything.
  • Stack sizes? Short stacks play for shove-or-fold; deep stacks can afford patience.
  • How big was the bet? A min-bet and an all-in ask completely different questions.
  • Who is betting? A rock-solid friend and a fearless bluffer deserve opposite answers.
  • What game is this? Cash game chips come back; tournament chips don't — survival weighs more.

The cheat sheets assume a normal friendly cash game. When the context changes, the right answer changes with it — the quiz will test you on exactly that.

Eight Ways Villains Win Your Chips

Every cartoon villain has a signature move. These are the eight signature leaks that keep friendly stacks moving around the table — learn them so nobody runs them on you.

  1. The Overeager Minion

    Playing every hand

    The leak
    "Any two cards! It's fun! I'm in!" — clicking every time the action reaches you, from every seat.
    Why it costs you
    Most hands miss the flop. Playing them all means paying, over and over, to lose slowly while the one good hand you do catch gets no respect.
    Cheat fix
    Ask before the flop: "Would I be happy showing this down?" If not, muck it.

  2. The Sleepy Guard

    Ignoring position

    The leak
    Playing the same hands from the first seat as from the last, as if seats were decoration.
    Why it costs you
    Early position means acting first with everyone still to come — you decide blind. Late position means acting last with full information. Same cards, wildly different jobs.
    Cheat fix
    Tighten up early, widen toward the button. When in doubt, act last.

  3. The Stubborn Henchman

    Chasing draws past reason

    The leak
    Four to a flush, four to a straight — calling any size bet all the way to the river on pure hope.
    Why it costs you
    Draws miss more often than they hit. Paying oversized bets to chase one turns a fun night into a slow bleed.
    Cheat fix
    Compare pot odds to your outs: nine outs with two cards to come wins roughly 35% of the time. If the price is steeper than your chances, fold.

  4. The Grumpy Boss

    Refusing to fold top pair

    The leak
    "I have a pair. I am not leaving. This is my pair now."
    Why it costs you
    Top pair with a weak kicker beats bluffs and loses to everything real. When a careful opponent keeps betting, it's often already beaten.
    Cheat fix
    Ask: "What am I beating if he fires again?" If the honest answer is "not much," fold and live to be dramatic tomorrow.

  5. The Phantom Trickster

    Bluffing every chance

    The leak
    Raising with nothing because "they'll never believe me this time."
    Why it costs you
    Friendly tables call a lot. A bluff that gets called is an expensive donation, and once you're caught, your real hands stop getting paid.
    Cheat fix
    Bluff less and bluff with a plan: in position, against one player who can actually fold, ideally with a draw that missed.

  6. The Passive Spy

    Always calling, never raising

    The leak
    Calling to "see what happens" — even with hands you genuinely like.
    Why it costs you
    You win small pots and lose big ones: you never build a pot with the best hand, and you never make draws pay to continue.
    Cheat fix
    Strong hand: raise for value. Nothing: usually fold. Treat "call" as an answer you earn, not a default you hide behind.

  7. Rage-Bot 3000

    Tilting after a bad beat

    The leak
    The river ruined everything, so now every hand is revenge.
    Why it costs you
    One lucky card by them doesn't make your next ten hands winners. Tilt just means they get paid twice — once when the card fell, and again when you shoved it back.
    Cheat fix
    Lose the hand, then take three breaths, a lap, or a snack before the next one.

  8. The Scriptless Director

    Betting without a plan

    The leak
    Pushing chips in before deciding why they're there.
    Why it costs you
    Every bet is either "worse hands, please call" (value) or "better hands, please fold" (bluff). If you can't say which, you're guessing with money.
    Cheat fix
    Start the sentence before the bet: "I'm betting because…" If it begins with "I hope…", check instead.
